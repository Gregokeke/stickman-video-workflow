## Main principle

Keep the explanatory scene in place and change the part that explains the current phrase. The reference builds information in layers: subject → context → variable → change → comparison → takeaway. Tiny internal actions carry the explanation instead of repeatedly sliding an entire finished picture onto the screen.

## Motion and hierarchy lessons

1. **Persistent context:** axes, labels, people and neutral reference columns remain while the highlighted data changes. Retained context lets viewers understand what changed.
2. **Independent parts:** gait, pointer position, column fills, stack units, legends and takeaway text have separate motion. A single exported screenshot would prevent the observed internal explanation.
3. **One attention target:** a selected column, changing fill boundary, growing stack or key number is dominant at any instant. Supporting elements remain quiet.
4. **Meaningful direction:** vertical growth measures quantity; horizontal progression follows ordered step categories; stacked units show a ratio; path drawing introduces a connection.
5. **Readable holds:** complete chart and ratio states remain available after the reveals. Continuous gait is a subject-specific action, not permission to bob every chart, word and prop.
6. **Selective emphasis:** skulls, the end expression, the exclamation and the pointer are brief explanatory punctuation. Do not accumulate decorative icons.
7. **Recreation timing, inferred:** short label/legend reveals can start around 0.2–0.4 s; a clear glide around 0.4–0.7 s; cumulative chart changes can develop across 1–2.5 s. Exact pacing must be retimed to the new voiceover, not copied as fixed presets.

## Combine this with the established stickman style

- Keep the existing rounded red-and-black ImageGen character family, white field, inward edge shading, faint silhouette shadows, upper captions and smooth move → hold → move choreography.
- Use a large character for the sarcastic hook or reaction. Move the character to a supporting left/right position when the demonstration becomes the main explanation. Return the character to prominence when the narration returns to acting or reaction.
- A pointing cane can be held naturally and aimed at the exact selected column, unit, threshold or label. Its tip must follow that target during movement. Generate a coherent held-cane pose or use genuinely separate usable parts; do not place a floating cane beside a hand.
- Keep the diagram as the hero during data explanations. The side character's face and gesture still need to read. Do not shrink both into tiny graphics to clear the interface.
- Use short, finite reactions: an eyebrow lift, skeptical lean, deadpan hold, glance at an absurd comparison, or a purposeful pointer movement. Humour belongs in timing and acting; it must not alter the numbers or suggest a different result.
- Draw actual charts, axes, bars and comparison units as editable precise elements when assembling. Use ImageGen for the principal character/illustrative assets. Keep chart values, captions and callouts controllable rather than baking an entire demonstration into one picture.
- Where named biological/chemical subjects occur, preserve the existing rule: depict the actual kind of entity and generate its identity label together with its illustration when helpful.
- Select clean whooshes for significant scene changes and short ticks/accents for selected milestones. A bar-by-bar reveal does not require a separate sound on every bar.
- Review the whole demonstration against the actual red-zone guide. Preserve the existing intentional oversized-character exception only where appropriate; protect the essential chart data, labels, cane tip and face.
