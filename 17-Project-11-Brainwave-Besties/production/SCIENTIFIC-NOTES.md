# Scientific sources and visual limits

The supplied recording remains verbatim. Illustrations and wave traces are explanatory schematics, not measured participant recordings.

Parkinson, Kleinbaum & Wheatley(2018), Similar neural responses predict friendship, Nature Communications9:332. DOI10.1038/s41467-017-02722-7. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-017-02722-7 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5790806/ . This primary study reports similar fMRI responses among friends viewing audiovisual movies. It does not establish literal Bluetooth communication, a shared single cell, or that friendship inevitably damages or downloads another person's neural wiring. Similarity is not proof of the narration's deterministic causal account. No invented sample results or percentages appear in the video.

The brainwave/scanner displays are labelled SCHEMATIC. Their ideal aligned waves illustrate the recording's language; they are not presented as authentic MRI/EEG traces. The claim of identical electrical spikes in the exact same millisecond is not independently validated here. Mirror-neuron drawings are representative neuron morphology; a unique mirror-neuron appearance is not implied. Amygdala and prefrontal models are anatomical illustrations, and empathy circuits are schematic.

The final download/rewiring passage uses a visible computing metaphor. Financial hardship or low ambition is not depicted as a diagnosed neurological injury or a proven source of literal brain damage. No new clinical data, diagnoses, research citations or treatment recommendations are fabricated to support the recording's rhetorical claims.
