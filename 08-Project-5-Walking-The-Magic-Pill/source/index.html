If Big Pharma dropped a
pill that cut your risk
of dying by 12%,
and let you stack another
12% for every extra pill
you popped with zero side
effects,
how many of it would
you snag for yourself?
Well,
that drug actually exists.
The bad news is,
it doesn't come in a
bottle,
so you can't wash it
down with a nice coffee
while rotting on the couch.
It's called walking,
something your legs were allegedly
designed to do.
Researchers tracked people's daily movement
and split them into groups.
Starting at the bottom with
the 3,000 step crowd,
whose only daily cardio is
shuffling to the front door
to grab Uber Eats,
all the way up to
the 16,000 step psychopaths,
who apparently have nowhere to
sit and zero existential dread
keeping them horizontal.
And they found that for
every extra 1,000 steps someone
took,
their risk of dying from
all causes dropped by 12%.
Every thousand steps,
you're basically bribing the Grim
Reaper to look the other
way.
But that's not even the
most offensive part of the
study.
When scientists compared the people
marinating at the bottom to
the ones at the top,
the least active group had
a 3-fold higher risk of
death.
Just to put into perspective
how absurd that is,
smoking cigarettes only increases your
risk of dying by about
2-fold compared to non-smokers.
Let that sink in.
Statistically,
rotting in your bed all
day is more lethal than
a pack a day habit.
Your mattress is literally outperforming
lung cancer.
Now,
I'm not saying you should
pick up smoking for your
health,
and you definitely don't need
to march 16,000 steps a
day like you're fleeing a
crime scene.
The average American barely scrapes
together 5,000 steps a day.
And the wild part is,
even if you just drag
your carcass from practically decomposing
down here,
up to mildly functional right
about here,
your risk of dying early
still plummets by over 50%.
You don't need 16,000 steps
a day to stay alive.
But forcing yourself to take
a walk from wherever you're
at right now is the
closest thing you'll ever get
to this magic pill.
Unless you're just really eager
to let six pallbearers do
the walking for you.
