# Sugar · Two Weeks — visual identity

## Style prompt
Rounded saturated red stickmen with bold black contours, expressive sassy eyebrows, deliberate hand language and coherent held-object acting. Bright white portrait canvas, soft gray inward edge shadows, faint directional silhouette shadows. No extra visual themes or colors. Match the approved Brain Overstimulation final interactive revision and its ImageGen family.

## Colors
Red #ED0B10, ink #101010, white #FFFFFF. Gray only for quiet inactive diagram context and framing shadows. Scientific subjects stay red/black/white.

## Typography
Approved local Caption Bold, stored in assets/fonts/caption-bold.ttf. Editable phrase captions near y285 with 740px width, normally 76px, reduced only to preserve two lines. Captions follow the supplied recording exactly. Identity labels are integrated in generated scientific assets; schematic qualifiers and diagram axes are editable.

## Composition
1080×1920, 30fps. Main hero usually occupies 700–1050px height. Clear diagonal hierarchy for pairs. Use actual alpha silhouettes, never stretch PNGs. Protect faces, grips, labels and cane tips from the supplied red-zone guide. The rare lower-target oversized presenter may crop only unimportant lower legs; record those ranges. No unnecessary duplicate legs, food props, chairs, pills or flash props.

## Props and hands — explicit user clarification
Keep the exact approved uploaded man holding the empty plate. Generate a hand-held/body-contact interaction together when the grip, contact and pose are integral. Generate props separately when they need independent arrival or movement: the refusing character and sugar bowl are separate layers. Both hands must have clean attachment and readable geometry.

## Motion
Smooth visible move → firm stick → purposeful move. Varied lateral and diagonal routes, rising details, moderate scale changes, retained center-to-side subjects and returns. No idle float, elastic bounce or random wobble. Separate travel/pose wrappers prevent competing transforms. Ordinary replacements fade early during incoming movement. Meaningful pairs remain as long as the relationship matters.

## Presenter
The generated cane is held coherently. Arrive after the main scientific object, above its artwork layer. Register the actual cane tip to the selected target. Move, enlarge and retarget when the explanation changes. Keep face/grip readable. No static presenter behind the diagram or pointing into empty space.

## Micro explanations
Use persistent axes and changing schematic lines for sugar rise/crash versus stability; a small insulin meter and a fuel path for the energy mechanism; a controlled glucose-to-collagen contact and signaling path. No invented clinical values. Explain with the changing part, rather than repeatedly replacing a finished chart screenshot.

## Sound
Use the user's new 72-effect pack and the original project whoosh. Action-aligned onsets, multiple whoosh/pop/rewind/ting/soft-air variants, selective solid contacts and quiet gaps. Gear sound supports the unlock/tap metaphor. No music, synthesized replacements or regenerated narration. Keep original, snapped voice, effects and final mix separate. Master for clear speech and audible midrange transients without clipping.

## What not to do
No generic code-drawn principal characters, crowded contact sheets, unrelated props, tiny unreadable people, opaque overlap tangles, arbitrary paths, duplicated filler, fake patient charts, literal gore, or extra color palette. Preserve the authoritative voiceover and full word endings.
